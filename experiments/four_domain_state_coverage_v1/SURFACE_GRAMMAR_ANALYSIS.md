# Grammar separately from semantic completeness

This is a post hoc CPU-only measurement, added after the formal behavior and identity probes. The original `grammar` flag also enforces controlled sentence/semantic completeness and some binding constraints. It is retained in all raw scores, admission, training and reported registered comparisons. The new measurement does not alter any model, output, current-source cohort or semantic success score.

`controlled_surface_grammar.py` reads emitted text only, imports no gold/world/renderer, and has three outcomes: all clauses match licensed finite English syntax with correct agreement/case; an identified clause has a clear agreement/case error; or unknown. It does not require task-specific facts, target relations or event participants to be present. Thus a facts-only response can be grammatical and semantically incomplete. Wrong roles, gender binding, date, sentiment degree and spatial relations can also be grammatical. Recognized metadata headers are licensed annotations, not English propositions; headers alone do not qualify as a complete body.

Unknown wording, actor names outside agreement-template coverage, unrecognized fragments and unmatched quotes remain unknown rather than confirmed ungrammatical. Limited lexical/frame coverage is not a general grammar judgment. The finite syntax accepts the declared generated sentence families and evaluates direct-quote sentence syntax separately. Agreement/case diagnostics have independent positive/negative fixtures, including grammatically valid but semantically incomplete/incorrect examples. It was developed with the data specification and fixed fixtures; original model grammar labels are not its targets.

`surface_grammar_by_seed.csv` covers all available natural formal, structural and position predictions, including failed admissions. It reports known-valid/identified-error/unknown counts, classified coverage, all-candidate known-valid lower-bound rate and validity conditional on classification. It never counts unknown as a confirmed grammar error or treats a semantic failure as a grammar error. Symbol strings have their separate parse validity and are excluded. This is a bounded automatic syntax audit, not independent human grammar labeling.
