\section{Predicting composability without decoding}\label{sec:prediction}
% CL8 CL8b CL9 CL12 CL15
\paragraph{Latent predictor.}\claim{CL8}
Given an operator or editor and a path, we run the chain on encoder states only and read the slot after every step with the locked vote
model; a world is predicted successful if the readout equals the gold state at every step. No decoding is involved. On fresh worlds of
the fifth round this predictor agrees with the observed chain outcome for \CLeightAgreeBart{} (BART) and \CLeightAgreeTfive{}
(T5Gemma) of the (world, cell) pairs, against \CLeightNaiveBart{} and \CLeightNaiveTfive{} for the naive prediction from step-1
success (lower bounds of the 95\% CI of the difference \CLeightDiffLoBart{} and \CLeightDiffLoTfive{}); it predicts the cell
category correctly for \CLeightCatBart{} and \CLeightCatTfive{} of the cells (Figure~\ref{fig:fig6}). Editors trained in that round,
whose behaviour was unknown when the predictions were locked, are predicted equally well (\CLeightNewBart{} and \CLeightNewTfive{}).
A per-cell majority reference, which uses the observed outcomes and is therefore not a predictor, reaches \CLeightMajBart{} and
\CLeightMajTfive{} on the same cells (exploratory post-hoc computation).

\paragraph{Exploratory: an equal-weight readout suffices.}\claim{CL8b}
Replacing the fitted plane weights by equal weights barely changes the agreement (\CLeightbEqualBart{} and \CLeightbEqualTfive{}), so the
predictor does not need decoder calibration.

\paragraph{Step-1 disagreement detects instruction writes.}\claim{CL9}
For trained editors, the fraction $D$ of worlds whose step-1 latent readout differs from the decoded state separates the two families
completely (Figure~\ref{fig:fig5}b): all \CLnineCE{} CE cells have $D\ge\Dhi$ and fail, all \CLnineState{} state-supervised cells have
$D\le\Dlo$. $D$ effectively detects whether the slot moved at step 1.

\paragraph{Failure modes.}\claim{CL12}
Predictions from idealized slot codes fail where chains drift. Decoupling the frozen plane from the dynamics by an oracle raises
one-way success in \CLtwelveUp{} of the \CLtwelveC{} cells where the standard freeze departs from the idealized prediction, but returns
the idealized category in only \CLtwelveIdealPct{}\% of them; the explanation by accumulated drift and readout margin is exploratory.

\paragraph{Where the predictor errs (exploratory).}\claim{CL15}
A post-hoc analysis of all state-supervised cells of the fifth and sixth rounds shows a clear structure in the errors. A predicted failure
is reliable: given that the predictor says a chain will fail, it fails in \CLfifteenPrecFailMin{}--\CLfifteenPrecFailMax{} of the worlds,
depending on backbone and domain. The errors are mostly over-estimates (\CLfifteenOver{} worlds predicted to compose that did not,
against \CLfifteenUnder{} the other way round); the imbalance is largest on unseen templates with T5Gemma (\CLfifteenTfiveOver{} against
\CLfifteenTfiveUnder{}). Where the slot still reads correctly but decoding fails, the first failure takes one of three forms. With
state-supervised editors trained for few updates, the decoder stays at the previous state (\CLfifteenPrevMin{}--\CLfifteenPrevMax{}\%
of such failures on one-way paths, BART month). With the fundamental frozen on unseen templates, it falls back to the start state
(\CLfifteenStartMin{}--\CLfifteenStartMax{}\%). With T5Gemma's alternating chains on unseen templates, the output is corrupted
(\CLfifteenUnparsable{}\% unparsable). We also asked whether clean state information in the context could explain the misses. In all
\CLfifteenProbes{} dev probes that put one state in the slot and another in the context, the decoder followed the slot, so a context
term calibrated on these probes receives weight zero and cannot change any prediction; on clean inputs the decoder reads the state
from the slot only. On the cells of editors trained for few updates the slot readout agrees with the observed outcome for
\CLfifteenCtOneAgree{} of the worlds (category accuracy \CLfifteenCtOneCat{}, \CLfifteenCtOneCells{} cells), against
\CLfifteenCtTwoAgree{} on a reference set of closed-form operators, surgeries and longer-trained editors. We do not use ``unconverged''
as a category: the preregistered criterion (dev canonical $L_2$ more than 20\% above that of RRR-16) does not separate composable from
non-composable editors; C3 with 600 updates composes on weekday (median one-way $\Rk{2N} = \CLfifteenWeekdaySixHundred{}$) while C3 with
900 updates does not on month ($\CLfifteenMonthNineHundred{}$), and both are ``unconverged'' by the criterion. The drift of the context
away from the canonical states separates the failures only weakly (AUC \CLfifteenDriftAucCtOne{}; \CLfifteenDriftAucCtTwo{} on the
reference set). \TODO{v7: rewrite with the preregistered U (margin and off-manifold checks) and R (repair) results.}
