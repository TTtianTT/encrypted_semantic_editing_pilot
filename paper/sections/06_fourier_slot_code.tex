\section{What is preserved: the Fourier code of the slot}\label{sec:fourier}
% CL7 CL5 CL6 CL10
\paragraph{Harmonic planes.}
Averaging the canonical slot encodings over worlds and removing the per-world mean gives $N$ points $\bar c(x)$. Projecting them on the
discrete Fourier modes of the cycle yields orthogonal \emph{harmonic planes} $B_k$ ($k=1,\dots,\lfloor N/2\rfloor$); the slot code is a
sum of rotations, one per plane, with angle $2\pi k x/N$.

\paragraph{Spectrum.}\claim{CL7}
Per plane we measure the amplitude $a_k$ and the one-step change $s_k$ of the slot code. The fundamental ($k=1$) changes least per
step: $s_1$ is the smallest in \CLsevenSoneSmallestAll{} of \CLsevenCellsAll{} (backbone, domain) cells, namely
\CLsevenSoneSmallest{} of \CLsevenCells{} in the preregistered fourth-round test and \CLsevenSoneSmallestVthree{} of
\CLsevenCellsVthree{} in an exploratory third-round analysis. The amplitude ordering is weaker: $a_1$ is the largest in
\CLsevenAoneLargestAll{} of \CLsevenACellsAll{} cells (\CLsevenAoneLargest{} of the \CLsevenAoneEvaluable{} fourth-round cells with
$N \ge 7$, where the test was defined, and \CLsevenAoneLargestVthree{} of \CLsevenACellsVthree{} third-round cells, exploratory). Every
exception is a parity mode: the $k=2$ mode of the two $\mathbb{Z}_4$ domains (egoside on BART, compass on T5Gemma) and the $k=4$ mode of
compass8 on T5Gemma (Figure~\ref{fig:fig3}). We use the symmetry argument of \citet{karkada2026symmetries} only to explain this spectrum; when
translation symmetry is broken the ordering can change.

\paragraph{Rank threshold and frozen planes.}\claim{CL5}
Closed-form RRR maps every state code exactly onto the next once its rank reaches the dimension of the centred code, $r^\star = N-1$
when all harmonics are present (Proposition~1, Appendix~\ref{app:theory}); under the readout a chain can still compose below this
rank when the dropped plane carries little vote weight. The threshold is sharp on \CLfiveRstarSharp{} domains of the second round.
Below it, least squares keeps the planes with the largest one-step energy per dimension (Proposition~2); because the fundamental changes
least, it is dropped first. Freezing
planes by surgery reproduces the signature (Figure~\ref{fig:fig2}): with the fundamental frozen, one-way chains fail, alternating chains
survive and one-way chains \emph{revive} at $t=N$, when the frozen fundamental is correct again; freezing every other plane gives the
mirror image. The fourth round confirmed these signatures in all \CLfiveVoneN{} and \CLfiveVtwoaN{} locked cells.

\paragraph{Multi-scale readout.}\claim{CL6}
The decoder reads the state as a nearest neighbour that weighs the planes: a vote model fitted on plane-substitution probes agrees
with the decoded state on \CLsixFullBart{} (BART) and \CLsixFullTfive{} (T5Gemma) of the steps of the fourth round's chains; equal
plane weights give \CLsixEqualBart{} and \CLsixEqualTfive{}, the fundamental alone only \CLsixFundBart{} and \CLsixFundTfive{}. The
multi-scale reading of these weights is exploratory.

\paragraph{Fundamental-first rank allocation.}\claim{CL10}
Because least squares sacrifices the fundamental first, reserving it fixes rank-deficient operators: on the \CLtenCells{} locked
T5Gemma cells ordinary RRR-$r$ reaches one-way $\Rk{2N}$ of at most \CLtenRRRMax{}, FF-$r$ at least \CLtenFFMin{}
(Figure~\ref{fig:fig4}); on BART the improvement is partial (exploratory).
