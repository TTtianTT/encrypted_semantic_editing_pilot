\section{What is written: instructions versus states}\label{sec:instructions}
% CL2 CL2b CL3 CL4
\paragraph{CE editors write instructions.}\claim{CL2}
The writes of C1 editors are highly similar across source states (median cosine \CLtwoWriteCosSource{} between writes from
different sources), although a high cosine still leaves a source-specific component (third-round erratum, Appendix~\ref{app:deviations});
they are unaligned with the canonical write $E(x\pm1)-E(x)$ (median cosine \CLtwoWriteCosCanon{}, of the order expected for unrelated
directions), and a linear probe on the edited slot still reads the \emph{source} state (median accuracy \CLtwoProbeSource{}). Taken
together, the edited slot keeps the source state and the edit does not move it towards the target encoding: the editor writes an
instruction that the decoder executes once. (We did not train a probe for the target state.) A second application adds the same instruction to a
latent that is no longer a canonical encoding, and the chain breaks. The fifth round's locked test on all \CLtwoCECells{} CE cells found a step-1 disagreement between the slot readout and the decoded
state in at least half of the worlds of every cell (Section~\ref{sec:prediction}); in the two editors we inspected, the slot readout
after one step is still the start state in \CLtwoSlotStaysBart{} of 160 worlds (BART weekday C1) and \CLtwoSlotStaysTfive{} of 160
(T5Gemma month C1-U4), while the decoder outputs the target.

\paragraph{Exploratory: the write geometry, with nulls.}\claim{CL26}
A ninth-round analysis of the same C1 editors (and of the natural-text editors) quantifies the picture. At least \CLtwentysixConstMin{}
of each world's write energy is the component shared by all source states, so the write is close to a constant. Its alignment with the
canonical write is no larger than that of random directions (median $|\cos|$ at most \CLtwentysixCosHi{}, against
\CLtwentysixNullLo{}--\CLtwentysixNullHi{} for random Gaussian directions), so ``unaligned'' means exactly that and nothing more. A
state probe trained on clean encodings reads the edited slot as the source state in at least \CLtwentysixSrcMin{} and as the target in at
most \CLtwentysixTgtMax{} of the cases. Independently of the training family, the step-1 slot readout equals the start state in every
world of every decoder-trained cell of the eighth round (minimum \CLtwentysixStartVeight{}; at least \CLtwentysixStartVfive{} per cell in
the fifth round).

\paragraph{Exploratory: relative or absolute instructions.}\claim{CL2b}
Exploratory analyses suggest that where the decoder natively performs the operation (weekday and month on BART; all three tested
domains on T5Gemma) the instruction is relative, and otherwise (scrambled maps, skip-two) it pushes towards a target token.
In a transplant analysis (fourth round) the write of a BART C1 editor, moved to another source state, still produced the relative
target in \CLtwobRelWeekday{} (weekday) and \CLtwobRelMonth{} (month) of the cases, but at most \CLtwobRelOtherMax{} on compass,
egoside and days-ago; on T5Gemma at least \CLtwobRelTfiveMin{} on every domain.

\paragraph{Unrolled CE learns a step-counting shortcut.}\claim{CL3}
Training CE editors along chains of up to four steps does not make them compose: they are almost exact inside the training horizon and
collapse one or two steps after it (Figure~\ref{fig:fig5}a). The seed-median one-way $\Rk{2N}$ of C1-U4 is at most
\CLthreeUoneMedianMax{} on every BART domain, and the result replicates on T5Gemma (C1-U4 $\Rk{2N}\le$ \CLthreeTfiveCOneUfour{}).
For compass ($N=4$) the horizon is exactly one turn of the cycle, so $\Rk{N}$ is perfect while $\Rk{2N}$ is zero.

\paragraph{State supervision makes the editors compose.}\claim{CL4}
Adding the canonical $L_2$ term to the unrolled objective (C3-U4) yields composable editors on both backbones (T5Gemma C3-U4
$\Rk{2N}\ge$ \CLfourTfiveCThreeUfour{}). With one-step training, composability depends on how far training has converged towards the
canonical write (exploratory). The default one-step C3 of 600 updates is therefore not guaranteed to
compose: one-step C3 on weekday composes after 600 updates but not after 450 (seed-median one-way
$\Rk{2N}$ \CLfourWeekSixHundred{} against \CLfourWeekFourFifty{}), while on month it needs 1200 (\CLfourMonthTwelveHundred{} against
\CLfourMonthNineHundred{} after 900).

\paragraph{Steering vectors.}\claim{CL20}
The editing methods that motivate this study often add a fixed vector to the representation~\citep{turner2023actadd,zou2023repe}. On a cyclic
attribute a constant write cannot implement a step: the mean difference $E(x+1)-E(x)$ averaged over all states cancels around the cycle,
and adding it decodes the target in at most \CLtwentyBoneaMax{} of worlds (ninth round, both backbones, weekday and month). The
difference of a single contrast pair works only from that pair's source state (\CLtwentyBonebXzero{}, against at most
\CLtwentyBonebOther{} from the other states). Once the added vector depends on the state read from the latent, the edit composes:
adding the mean difference of the read state gives one-way $\Rk{2N}$ of at least \CLtwentyBonecMin{}. This readout-indexed steering is
not an affine map; it is a state write by construction.

\paragraph{Objective, not parameterisation or capacity.}\claim{CL21}
Instruction writes are not specific to our editor's parameterisation. A LoReFT intervention~\citep{wu2024reft}, a low-rank affine
intervention of the same family, trained with the one-step objective succeeds at step 1 (\CLtwentyoneLConeRone{}), fails at step 2
($\Rk{2} \le \CLtwentyoneLConeRtwo{}$, one-way $\Rk{2N} \le \CLtwentyoneLConeRtwoN{}$) and shows the step-1 disagreement of an instruction
write ($D \ge \CLtwentyoneLConeD{}$); trained with the state-supervised unrolled objective it composes in three of the four combinations
(\CLtwentyoneLCthreeMin{}) but not on T5Gemma month (\CLtwentyoneLCthreeTfive{}; a condition removed before the lock). A full-rank linear
editor and an MLP editor trained with the one-step objective behave like C1 ($\Rk{2} \le \CLtwentyoneCapRtwo{}$, $D \ge
\CLtwentyoneCapD{}$, $\Rk{2N} \le \CLtwentyoneCapRtwoN{}$): more capacity does not turn an instruction into a state.

\paragraph{Longer and random horizons.}\claim{CL22}
Unrolled training with the decoder objective alone over longer horizons (up to 8, up to 16, or random up to $2N$ steps) mostly fails to
learn its own horizon: \CLtwentytwoAdmitted{} of \CLtwentytwoTrained{} editors reached the admission criterion. The editors that did
(BART weekday) fail past their horizon (median $\Rk{S_{\max}+2} = \CLtwentytwoUeight{}$) and none composes over $4N$ steps
($\Rk{4N} \le \CLtwentytwoRfourN{}$); the other combinations are descriptive.

\paragraph{Seeds.}\claim{CL24}
On eight seeds (five new ones and the fifth round's three) the main editor results hold in all \CLtwentyfourCells{} cells: C1 has
$\Rk{2} \le \CLtwentyfourCOneRtwoMax{}$ for every seed, and C3-U4 one-way $\Rk{2N} \ge \CLtwentyfourCThreeMin{}$ for every seed.
