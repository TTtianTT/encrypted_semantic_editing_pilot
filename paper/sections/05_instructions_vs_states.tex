\section{What is written: instructions versus states}\label{sec:instructions}
% CL2 CL2b CL3 CL4
\paragraph{CE editors write instructions.}\claim{CL2}
The writes of C1 editors are nearly identical across source states (median cosine \CLtwoWriteCosSource{} between writes from
different sources) and nearly orthogonal to the canonical write $E(x\pm1)-E(x)$ (median cosine \CLtwoWriteCosCanon{}); a linear probe
on the edited slot still reads the \emph{source} state (median accuracy \CLtwoProbeSource{}). The edited latent therefore does not
encode the target state; it encodes an instruction that the decoder executes once. A second application adds the same instruction to a
latent that is no longer a canonical encoding, and the chain breaks. The fifth round confirms the signature on all
\CLtwoCECells{} CE cells: after one step the latent readout of the slot is still the start state (for example in
\CLtwoSlotStaysBart{} of 160 worlds for BART weekday C1 and \CLtwoSlotStaysTfive{} of 160 for T5Gemma month C1-U4), while the decoder
outputs the target.

\paragraph{Exploratory: relative or absolute instructions.}\claim{CL2b}
Exploratory analyses suggest that where the decoder natively performs the operation (weekday and month on BART; all three tested
domains on T5Gemma) the instruction is relative, and otherwise (scrambled maps, skip-two) it pushes towards a target token.
In a transplant analysis (fourth round) the write of a BART C1 editor, moved to another source state, still produced the relative
target in \CLtwobRelWeekday{} (weekday) and \CLtwobRelMonth{} (month) of the cases, but at most \CLtwobRelOtherMax{} on compass,
egoside and days-ago; on T5Gemma at least \CLtwobRelTfiveMin{} on every domain.

\paragraph{Unrolled CE learns a step-counting shortcut.}\claim{CL3}
Training CE editors along chains of up to four steps does not make them compose: they are almost exact inside the training horizon and
collapse one or two steps after it (Figure~\ref{fig:fig5}a). The seed-median one-way $\Rk{2N}$ of C1-U4 is at most
\CLthreeUoneMedianMax{} on every BART domain, and the result replicates on T5Gemma (C1-U4 $\Rk{2N}\le$ \CLthreeTfiveCOneUfour{}).
For compass ($N=4$) the horizon is exactly one turn of the cycle, so $\Rk{N}$ is perfect while $\Rk{2N}$ is zero.

\paragraph{State supervision makes the editors compose.}\claim{CL4}
Adding the canonical $L_2$ term to the unrolled objective (C3-U4) yields composable editors on both backbones (T5Gemma C3-U4
$\Rk{2N}\ge$ \CLfourTfiveCThreeUfour{}). With one-step training, composability depends on how far training has converged towards the
canonical write (exploratory): one-step C3 on weekday composes after 600 updates but not after 450 (seed-median one-way
$\Rk{2N}$ \CLfourWeekSixHundred{} against \CLfourWeekFourFifty{}), while on month it needs 1200 (\CLfourMonthTwelveHundred{} against
\CLfourMonthNineHundred{} after 900).
