\paragraph{Symmetry is not necessary.}\claim{CL11}
Composability does not require the states to be laid out symmetrically. Scrambling the order of the state words, so that the encoder's
cycle no longer matches the operation, still gives composable closed-form operators in \CLelevenScrComposable{} of
\CLelevenScrTotal{} cells; what changes is the rank needed (for scrambled month $r^\star = \CLelevenRstarScrMonth{}$ instead of
$N-1$). At the rank threshold, long natural chains are not more stable than scrambled ones (difference in $\Rk{10N}$ of
\CLelevenMonthDiff{} on month).

\paragraph{Unseen templates.}\claim{CL14}
All results above use a fixed sentence template. In the sixth round we evaluated on held-out templates never seen when fitting or
training. A closed-form operator restricted to the slot position composes on them: one-way $\Rk{2N}$ is \CLfourteenBaseWeekday{},
\CLfourteenBaseMonth{} and \CLfourteenBaseHour{} on BART (weekday, month, hour) and \CLfourteenBaseTfive{} on T5Gemma (month; Figure~\ref{fig:figS1}), whereas
the full-sequence operator of the previous round failed on the training templates already. The latent predictor still works on BART
(agreement \CLfourteenAgreeBart{} against \CLfourteenNaiveBart{} for the naive prediction, lower CI bound of the difference
\CLfourteenDiffLoBart{}, category accuracy \CLfourteenCatBart{}). The preregistered overall verdict is nevertheless mixed, for two
reasons. On T5Gemma the predictor over-estimates several cells (category accuracy \CLfourteenCatTfive{}, agreement
\CLfourteenAgreeTfive{}). And on held-out templates alternating chains are weaker than one-way chains, already for the unfrozen
operator, so the frozen-fundamental test misses its alternating threshold on weekday (\CLfourteenFoneAltWeekday{}) while passing on
month (\CLfourteenFoneAltMonth{}). Trained CE editors generalise less: the full-sequence C1 succeeds in one step on only
\CLfourteenConeRoneWeekday{}--\CLfourteenConeRoneHour{} of held-out BART worlds (T5Gemma \CLfourteenConeRoneTfive{}) and fails at
step 2 everywhere. Three qualifications apply. First, text-level deduplication against all earlier renders exhausted the held-out
templates with few fields, so these results speak for templates with several fields (\CLfourteenTmplSparse{} sparse templates carry at
most \CLfourteenTmplSparseMax{} worlds; Appendix~\ref{app:data}). Second, when T5Gemma's alternating chains fail on unseen templates the
output is mostly corrupted rather than a wrong state (\CLfifteenUnparsable{}\% unparsable; Section~\ref{sec:prediction}). Third, the
frozen-fundamental test used an absolute alternating threshold although the unfrozen operator itself was below it; a threshold relative
to the unfrozen operator would have been the right design (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Alternating chains on unseen templates.}\claim{CL16}
The seventh round asked why alternating chains are weaker on unseen templates. On BART-base the failures of the unfrozen operator are
in-plane: restoring the slot's in-plane position alone repairs \CLfifteenRAbartPush{} of them (secondary prediction). The latent
trajectories show where the error goes: on all \CLsixteenCombos{} (backbone, domain) combinations the off-manifold part of the slot falls
within one one-way cycle ($\rho(N)/\rho(1)$ between \CLsixteenNOneMin{} and \CLsixteenNOneMax{}), whereas on alternating chains it
accumulates to \CLsixteenAltMin{}--\CLsixteenAltMax{} times the one-way value after $2N$ steps. A first-order account in which one-way
errors cancel over a cycle (Proposition~4, Appendix~\ref{app:theory}) fits the training templates (cycle-sum ratio
\CLsixteenCycDevMin{}--\CLsixteenCycDevMax{}, exploratory) but not the held-out ones (\CLsixteenCycMin{}--\CLsixteenCycMax{}), so we
keep it as an unconfirmed explanation. Pairing the operator with its exact inverse does not help: used as the minus operator, the
inverse fails from the first steps ($\Rk{2N} = \CLsixteenInvMax{}$ on every combination). \TODO{E1 (v8): whether the fitted operators
contract and the exact inverse expands off-manifold directions.}

\paragraph{Binding.}\claim{CL13}
Binding domains, where the slot must point to the next of three listed participants, were the hardest case in earlier rounds: the best
attention editor reached $\Rk{6} = \CLthirteenBtwo{}$ and a token-wise editor \CLthirteenTokU{}. A two-layer attention editor trained
with multi-step canonical supervision on unrolls of up to eight steps survives 30 steps on \CLthirteenSurvivors{} of eight seeds
(at least 90\% of worlds correct through step 30), but every seed decays by 100 steps ($\Rk{100} \le \CLthirteenHundredMax{}$; Figure~\ref{fig:figS2}). The result we
reported after the sixth round, three seeds at $\Rk{30}$ of \CLthirteenSeedA{}, \CLthirteenSeedB{} and \CLthirteenSeedC{}, did not
replicate on new seeds (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). An oracle-routing editor trained on the same eight-step horizon also decays
(median $\Rk{30} = \CLthirteenOrEightThirty{}$), so the failure is not only a matter of learning the route. The write error grows
steadily: the distance between the slot and the clean encoding of the gold state is about \CLthirteenDistTen{} slot radii after 10
steps, \CLthirteenDistThirtyLo{}--\CLthirteenDistThirtyHi{} after 30 and \CLthirteenDistHundredLo{}--\CLthirteenDistHundredHi{} after
100 for the seeds that survive 30 steps (exploratory). The lookup itself happens in the first layer: in \CLthirteenLayerOneCount{} of
eight seeds the slot attends to the next listed name with weight \CLthirteenLayerOneLo{}--\CLthirteenLayerOneHi{} (exploratory). We read
the bottleneck as write fidelity rather than routing, an interpretation that E2 (v8) tests. Stability also varies across domains: on
name3 one sixth-round seed reaches only \CLthirteenNameSeedB{}.
