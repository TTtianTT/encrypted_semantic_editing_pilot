\paragraph{A translation-symmetric ordering of the state words is not necessary.}\claim{CL11}
Composability does not require the states to be laid out symmetrically. Scrambling the order of the state words, so that the encoder's
cycle no longer matches the operation, still gives composable closed-form operators in \CLelevenScrComposable{} of
\CLelevenScrTotal{} cells; what changes is the rank needed (for scrambled month $r^\star = \CLelevenRstarScrMonth{}$ instead of
$N-1$). At the rank threshold, long natural chains are not more stable than scrambled ones (difference in $\Rk{10N}$ of
\CLelevenMonthDiff{} on month).

\paragraph{Unseen templates.}\claim{CL14}
All results above use a fixed sentence template. In the sixth round we evaluated on held-out templates never seen when fitting or
training. A closed-form operator restricted to the slot position composes on them: one-way $\Rk{2N}$ is \CLfourteenBaseWeekday{},
\CLfourteenBaseMonth{} and \CLfourteenBaseHour{} on BART (weekday, month, hour) and \CLfourteenBaseTfive{} on T5Gemma (month; Figure~\ref{fig:figS1}), whereas
the full-sequence operator of the previous round failed on the training templates already. The latent predictor still works on BART
(agreement \CLfourteenAgreeBart{} against \CLfourteenNaiveBart{} for the naive prediction, lower CI bound of the difference
\CLfourteenDiffLoBart{}, category accuracy \CLfourteenCatBart{}). The preregistered overall verdict is nevertheless mixed, for two
reasons. On T5Gemma the predictor over-estimates several cells (category accuracy \CLfourteenCatTfive{}, agreement
\CLfourteenAgreeTfive{}). And on held-out templates alternating chains are weaker than one-way chains, already for the unfrozen
operator, so the frozen-fundamental test misses its alternating threshold on weekday (\CLfourteenFoneAltWeekday{}) while passing on
month (\CLfourteenFoneAltMonth{}). Trained CE editors generalise less: the full-sequence C1 succeeds in one step on only
\CLfourteenConeRoneWeekday{}--\CLfourteenConeRoneHour{} of held-out BART worlds (T5Gemma \CLfourteenConeRoneTfive{}) and fails at
step 2 everywhere. Three qualifications apply. First, text-level deduplication against all earlier renders exhausted the held-out
templates with few fields, so these results speak for templates with several fields (\CLfourteenTmplSparse{} sparse templates carry at
most \CLfourteenTmplSparseMax{} worlds; Appendix~\ref{app:data}). Second, when T5Gemma's alternating chains fail on unseen templates the
output is mostly corrupted rather than a wrong state (\CLfifteenUnparsable{}\% unparsable; Section~\ref{sec:prediction}). Third, the
frozen-fundamental test used an absolute alternating threshold although the unfrozen operator itself was below it; a threshold relative
to the unfrozen operator would have been the right design (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Alternating chains on unseen templates.}\claim{CL16}
The seventh round asked why alternating chains are weaker on unseen templates. On BART-base the failures of the unfrozen operator are
in-plane: restoring the slot's in-plane position alone repairs \CLfifteenRAbartPush{} of them (secondary prediction). The latent
trajectories show where the error goes: on all \CLsixteenCombos{} (backbone, domain) combinations the off-manifold part of the slot falls
within one one-way cycle ($\rho(N)/\rho(1)$ between \CLsixteenNOneMin{} and \CLsixteenNOneMax{}), whereas on alternating chains it
accumulates to \CLsixteenAltMin{}--\CLsixteenAltMax{} times the one-way value after $2N$ steps. A first-order account in which one-way
errors cancel over a cycle (Proposition~4, Appendix~\ref{app:theory}) fits the training templates (cycle-sum ratio
\CLsixteenCycDevMin{}--\CLsixteenCycDevMax{}, exploratory) but not the held-out ones (\CLsixteenCycMin{}--\CLsixteenCycMax{}), so we
keep it as an unconfirmed explanation. Pairing the operator with its exact inverse does not help: used as the minus operator, the
inverse fails from the first steps ($\Rk{2N} = \CLsixteenInvMax{}$ on every combination).
An exploratory analysis of the operators' linear parts (eighth round, before its lock; no decoding) points to why. Along the chains, the
fitted plus and minus operators shrink the off-manifold part of the slot at every step (median gain \CLeoneSlotPLo{}--\CLeoneSlotPHi{}
for plus, \CLeoneSlotMLo{}--\CLeoneSlotMHi{} for minus, on all four combinations and both path types), while the exact inverse
enlarges it (\CLeoneSlotPinvLo{}--\CLeoneSlotPinvHi{}). Least squares thus fits a slightly contracting map, which is consistent with
the one-way decrease of $\rho$ above; inverting it turns the contraction into expansion, which is consistent with the failure of the
exact inverse. For the template-0 RRR-16 control the plus and minus gains are close to one (\CLeoneCtlPLo{}--\CLeoneCtlPHi{} and
\CLeoneCtlMLo{}--\CLeoneCtlMHi{}). This is an explanation, not a tested prediction.

\paragraph{Natural text.}\claim{CL17}
The eighth round replaced the templates with \CLseventeenWorlds{} sentences from Wikipedia articles (WikiText-103) that mention one month,
each reconstructed exactly by both backbones with every month substituted (Appendix~\ref{app:data}). The preregistered verdict is that
the findings hold on natural text for both backbones. The slot-centred operator fitted on natural training sentences composes (one-way
$\Rk{2N}$ \CLseventeenNoneBart{} on BART-base and \CLseventeenNoneTfive{} on T5Gemma), and the operator fitted on the synthetic templates
of the sixth round transfers without refitting (\CLseventeenSynBart{} and \CLseventeenSynTfive{}; descriptive, because it already
composed on the natural dev sentences). Decoder-trained editors again write instructions: step-1 success is at least
\CLseventeenCOneRoneMin{}, $\Rk{2}$ at most \CLseventeenCOneRtwoMax{}, and the step-1 disagreement $D$ at least \CLseventeenDMin{}. The
freeze signatures hold: with the fundamental frozen, one-way chains fail on BART-base (\CLseventeenFoneBart{}; on T5Gemma
\CLseventeenFoneTfive{}, a condition removed before the lock because the latent model itself put it on the wrong side), alternating chains
survive (at least \CLseventeenFoneAltMin{}), and freezing every other plane fails (at most \CLseventeenFnotMax{}). The latent predictor
agrees with the decoded chains for \CLseventeenAgreeBart{} and \CLseventeenAgreeTfive{} of (world, cell) pairs, against
\CLseventeenNaiveBart{} and \CLseventeenNaiveTfive{} for the naive rule; the per-cell majority reference reaches
\CLseventeenMajBart{} and \CLseventeenMajTfive{}, so on T5Gemma nearly all of the gain is at the level of cells.

\paragraph{Scale.}\claim{CL18}
On T5Gemma 9B-9B (weekday and month, template 0, evaluated for the first time on this model) five of the six preregistered tests hold:
the instruction shortcut ($\Rk{2} \le \CLeighteenCOneRtwoMax{}$ with $D$ separating all \CLeighteenDsepCells{} editor cells), the freeze
signatures, the spectrum ($s_1$ smallest), latent prediction (\CLeighteenAgree{} against \CLeighteenNaive{}) and composition of the
state-supervised unrolled editor ($\Rk{2N} \ge \CLeighteenCThreeMin{}$). The rank test holds on weekday ($r^\star =
\CLeighteenRstarWeekday{}$ as predicted) and fails on month: rank \CLeighteenRstarMonth{} composes as Proposition~1 predicts, but the
rank-deficient operators do not degrade monotonically (RRR-9 \CLeighteenRnineMonth{}, RRR-10 \CLeighteenRtenMonth{}), so the lower side of
the threshold is not met. Reserving rank for the fundamental does not repair these operators on 9B-9B (FF-9 \CLeighteenFFnineMonth{},
FF-10 \CLeighteenFFtenMonth{}; descriptive), unlike on T5Gemma 2B-2B. Table~\ref{tab:scale} adds the smaller T5Gemma sizes
(exploratory, closed-form operators only): they repeat the weekday picture, while on L-L month no operator up to rank 16 composes
(RRR-16 \CLeighteenLLmonth{}), and B-B month was skipped because the clean sentences did not reconstruct (\CLeighteenBBmonthRecon{}).

\paragraph{Decoder-only models (exploratory).}\claim{CL19}
In the residual stream of Gemma 2 2B (the decoder-only model T5Gemma 2B-2B was adapted from), with the model asked to repeat the sentence
and the month latent replaced after layer \CLnineteenLayer{} (Appendix~\ref{app:implementation}), the same structure appears: the slot operator
composes from rank \CLnineteenRstar{} on, freezing the fundamental breaks one-way chains (\CLnineteenFonePlus{}) but not alternating ones
(\CLnineteenFoneAlt{}), and a decoder-trained editor writes an instruction ($\Rk{2} \le \CLnineteenCOneRtwoMax{}$, $D \ge
\CLnineteenDMin{}$). The readout agrees with decoding in at least \CLnineteenAgreeMin{} of worlds per closed-form cell. Layers 2 and 18
give the same picture.

\paragraph{Binding.}\claim{CL13}
Binding domains, where the slot must point to the next of three listed participants, were the hardest case in earlier rounds: the best
attention editor reached $\Rk{6} = \CLthirteenBtwo{}$ and a token-wise editor \CLthirteenTokU{}. A two-layer attention editor trained
with multi-step canonical supervision on unrolls of up to eight steps survives 30 steps on \CLthirteenSurvivors{} of eight seeds
(at least 90\% of worlds correct through step 30), but every seed decays by 100 steps ($\Rk{100} \le \CLthirteenHundredMax{}$; Figure~\ref{fig:figS2}). The result we
reported after the sixth round, three seeds at $\Rk{30}$ of \CLthirteenSeedA{}, \CLthirteenSeedB{} and \CLthirteenSeedC{}, did not
replicate on new seeds (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}). An oracle-routing editor trained on the same eight-step horizon also decays
(median $\Rk{30} = \CLthirteenOrEightThirty{}$), so the failure is not only a matter of learning the route. The write error grows
steadily: the distance between the slot and the clean encoding of the gold state is about \CLthirteenDistTen{} slot radii after 10
steps, \CLthirteenDistThirtyLo{}--\CLthirteenDistThirtyHi{} after 30 and \CLthirteenDistHundredLo{}--\CLthirteenDistHundredHi{} after
100 for the seeds that survive 30 steps (exploratory). The lookup itself happens in the first layer: in \CLthirteenLayerOneCount{} of
eight seeds the slot attends to the next listed name with weight \CLthirteenLayerOneLo{}--\CLthirteenLayerOneHi{} (exploratory). Raising the oracle editor's write rank from 32 to
128 or full rank does not help (eighth round, exploratory): median $\Rk{30}$ stays at most \CLthirteenEtwoRthirtyMax{}, and after 100
steps the slot is further from the gold encoding at full rank (\CLthirteenEtwoDistFullLo{}--\CLthirteenEtwoDistFullHi{} slot radii)
than at rank 32 (\CLthirteenEtwoDistLo{}--\CLthirteenEtwoDistHi{}). At every rank the write keeps where the chain came from: on chains
whose direction is random, a linear probe reads the last step's direction from the slot with accuracy at least \CLthirteenEtwoDirMin{}
within the training horizon (chance one half). We therefore cannot attribute the decay to the write rank; its cause remains unexplained. Stability also varies across domains: on
name3 one sixth-round seed reaches only \CLthirteenNameSeedB{}.

\paragraph{Other limits.}
Encoder--decoder backbones up to 9B parameters, with one decoder-only model explored; single-token states; natural text only for the
month attribute; greedy decoding.
The contrast of Section~\ref{sec:phenomenon} is between fitting objectives: a one-step decoder objective does not ask the edited latent to
stay among encodings, and the closed-form operator is fitted on encodings, so the failure of C1 is the expected baseline; what the
experiments add is what the CE editors write instead (Section~\ref{sec:instructions}) and which structure a state write must carry
(Section~\ref{sec:fourier}). We did not compare with mean-difference steering vectors, intervention methods such as ReFT, longer or
randomised unrolling horizons, or full-rank and nonlinear editors.
Any $N$ centred points decompose into harmonic planes; the content of the Fourier account is which planes carry the state and how
the editors treat them, and a scrambled state order composes at a different rank (CL11), so the account describes these domains rather
than a requirement of every composable edit. Trained-editor results rest on three seeds (eight for binding, where the three-seed result
of the sixth round did not replicate).
The latent predictor's vote weights are calibrated on decoded dev probes, and the one-step test decodes the first step; neither
decodes the confirmation chains whose outcome they predict. Part of the predictor's agreement is at the level of cells: a reference that
predicts each cell's majority outcome, which needs the decoded outcomes, reaches \CLeightMajBart{} and \CLeightMajTfive{} (exploratory).
