\paragraph{Symmetry is not necessary.}\claim{CL11}
Composability does not require the states to be laid out symmetrically. Scrambling the order of the state words, so that the encoder's
cycle no longer matches the operation, still gives composable closed-form operators in \CLelevenScrComposable{} of
\CLelevenScrTotal{} cells; what changes is the rank needed (for scrambled month $r^\star = \CLelevenRstarScrMonth{}$ instead of
$N-1$). At the rank threshold, long natural chains are not more stable than scrambled ones (difference in $\Rk{10N}$ of
\CLelevenMonthDiff{} on month).

\paragraph{Unseen templates.}\claim{CL14}
All results above use a fixed sentence template. In the sixth round we evaluated on held-out templates never seen when fitting or
training. A closed-form operator restricted to the slot position composes on them: one-way $\Rk{2N}$ is \CLfourteenBaseWeekday{},
\CLfourteenBaseMonth{} and \CLfourteenBaseHour{} on BART (weekday, month, hour) and \CLfourteenBaseTfive{} on T5Gemma (month; Figure~\ref{fig:figS1}), whereas
the full-sequence operator of the previous round failed on the training templates already. The latent predictor still works on BART
(agreement \CLfourteenAgreeBart{} against \CLfourteenNaiveBart{} for the naive prediction, lower CI bound of the difference
\CLfourteenDiffLoBart{}, category accuracy \CLfourteenCatBart{}). The preregistered overall verdict is nevertheless mixed, for two
reasons. On T5Gemma the predictor over-estimates several cells (category accuracy \CLfourteenCatTfive{}, agreement
\CLfourteenAgreeTfive{}). And on held-out templates alternating chains are weaker than one-way chains, already for the unfrozen
operator, so the frozen-fundamental test misses its alternating threshold on weekday (\CLfourteenFoneAltWeekday{}) while passing on
month (\CLfourteenFoneAltMonth{}). Trained CE editors generalise less: the full-sequence C1 succeeds in one step on only
\CLfourteenConeRoneWeekday{}--\CLfourteenConeRoneHour{} of held-out BART worlds (T5Gemma \CLfourteenConeRoneTfive{}) and fails at
step 2 everywhere.

\paragraph{Binding.}\claim{CL13}
Binding domains, where the slot must point to the next of three listed participants, were the hardest case in earlier rounds: the best
attention editor reached $\Rk{6} = \CLthirteenBtwo{}$ and a token-wise editor \CLthirteenTokU{}. With a two-layer attention editor
trained with multi-step canonical supervision on unrolls of up to eight steps, binding edits compose: median $\Rk{30} =
\CLthirteenAttTwoLEightThirty{}$ on bind3 (Figure~\ref{fig:figS2}). The same editor trained on unrolls of up to four steps reaches only
\CLthirteenAttTwoLFourThirty{}, and a one-layer editor trained on eight-step unrolls decays after its horizon ($\Rk{8} =
\CLthirteenAttOneEightEight{}$, $\Rk{12} = \CLthirteenAttOneEightTwelve{}$, $\Rk{30} = \CLthirteenAttOneEightThirty{}$). An editor
with oracle routing to the next participant, a diagnostic, decays in the same way after its four-step training horizon ($\Rk{8} =
\CLthirteenOrEight{}$, $\Rk{12} = \CLthirteenOrTwelve{}$, $\Rk{30} = \CLthirteenOrThirty{}$); by the preregistered rule this
counts against representability, but together with the two-layer result the decay reads as a horizon effect. Stability is not
uniform: on the reused bind3fix worlds the four-step two-layer editor reaches only \CLthirteenFixAttTwoLFour{}. How the two-layer
editor implements the lookup is open: its second layer puts almost no attention on the participant positions (exploratory).
