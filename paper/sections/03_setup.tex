\section{Setup}\label{sec:setup}
% CL1 (definitions only)
\paragraph{Backbones and latent edits.}
We use two frozen encoder--decoder models, BART-base and T5Gemma (2B--2B, bf16). An input sentence $x$ is encoded into a sequence of
encoder states $h = E(x)$; an edit is a map $T: h \mapsto h'$ applied to the encoder states, after which the frozen decoder generates
text from $h'$. Neither the encoder nor the decoder is ever updated; only the editors are trained or fitted.

\paragraph{Domains.}
Each \emph{world} fixes the surface content of a template sentence (participants, an object, its colour, quantity and status) and an
attribute with $N$ cyclically ordered states: weekday ($N=7$), month ($N=12$), compass and season ($N=4$), compass8 ($N=8$) and hour
($N=12$). The operations \emph{plus} and \emph{minus} move the state by $\pm1 \bmod N$. The state word is a single token in every
template, and the encoder position that holds it is the \emph{slot}. Binding domains instead store which of three listed participants
the attribute points to (bind3: a per-world list; bind3fix: a fixed list; name3: three fixed names outside the list).

\paragraph{Editors.}
\emph{Closed-form operators}: reduced-rank regression RRR-$r$ fitted on canonical pairs $(E(x), E(x\pm1))$ of the training worlds, and
its fundamental-first variant FF-$r$, which reserves two of the $r$ directions for the slowest Fourier mode of the slot code
(Section~\ref{sec:fourier}). \emph{Trained editors} are low-rank residual maps trained against the frozen decoder: C1 minimises the
decoder cross-entropy of the target sentence after one step; C3 adds a canonical $L_2$ term pulling $T(E(x))$ towards $E(x\pm1)$;
unrolled variants (U4) are trained along chains of up to four steps. C1, C3, C3-U4 and RRR-$r$ are all low-rank affine residual maps
applied to every token, $h_i \mapsto h_i + U V h_i + b$; they differ only in how they are fitted. For binding we also use token-wise and
attention editors.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
A chain applies $k$ operations from the world's start state and decodes after every step. $\Rk{k}$ is the fraction of worlds whose
decoded sentence is correct at every step $1,\dots,k$; $A_t$ is the per-step accuracy at step $t$. We use one-way chains (plus or minus,
$2N$ steps) and alternating chains (plus, minus, \dots). A cell is \emph{composable} if its one-way $\Rk{2N}$ is high; we report the
categories composable / partial / fail with the locked thresholds. The step-1 disagreement $D$ (Section~\ref{sec:prediction}) compares
the latent readout of the slot with the decoded state after one step.

\paragraph{Preregistration.}
The work proceeded in \CLmetaRounds{} preregistered rounds. In each round the predictions, thresholds and decision rules were locked (a git commit
recorded in every claim) before any confirmation-split output was decoded; confirmation worlds were freshly generated and text-level
deduplicated against all earlier renders\footnote{The second round deduplicated its confirmation worlds only among themselves; the third round's audit found that \CLmetaReuseMin{}--\CLmetaReuseMax{}\% of them per domain rendered to a text also present in the first round's training or dev split. The second round's closed-form results were at ceiling, and every later round deduplicated against all earlier renders. Re-running the locked analyses of the first two rounds without the
duplicated worlds (fourth round) left every recomputed verdict unchanged; the second round's write-geometry statistics (the cosines
and probe of Section~\ref{sec:instructions}) were stored only as summaries and could not be recomputed (Appendix~\ref{app:deviations}).};
sanity gates had to pass before evaluation; confidence intervals are world-cluster bootstraps (2000 resamples). Every prediction,
including failed, mixed and non-evaluable ones, is listed in Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}.
