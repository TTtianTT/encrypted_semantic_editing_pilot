\section{Data and templates}\label{app:data}
% definitions for CL1, CL13, CL14
\paragraph{Worlds and splits.}
A world fixes every surface field of a template sentence except the state. Each cyclic domain has $96$ train and $32$ dev worlds and,
for every round that evaluates it, $160$ freshly generated confirmation worlds. Confirmation worlds are text-level deduplicated against
every render of the domain in all earlier rounds and splits (since v3; Appendix~\ref{app:deviations} for v2). Every world is rendered in
all $N$ states; a render is the gold text of a state.

\paragraph{Single-template domains.}
Template~0 is the main template for every cyclic domain; it places the state word as a single token at a fixed encoder position (the
slot), which the audit checks per world. Template~3, which starts with a person's name, is used for the binding domains from v3 on
because it passes the reconstruction gate on BART (Appendix~\ref{app:protocol}).

\paragraph{Binding domains.}
bind3 lists three participants per world and the attribute points to one of them; bind3fix uses a fixed list; name3 stores one of three
fixed names that are not in the list, and so needs no routing.

\paragraph{Multi-template domains (v5, v6).}
weekday-mt, month-mt and hour-mt render the state inside one of $36$ domain-independent sentence skeletons (the slot phrase is ``on X'',
``in X'' and ``at X o'clock''). Templates were frozen before any model run and could only be removed: those whose state word was not a single token at a constant slot position, or that failed the reconstruction gate on train and dev, were dropped, leaving $29$; they are split,
stratified by the presence of a distractor word, into $20$ training and $9$ held-out templates. Each world uses one template; the
multi-template domains have $192$ train, $48$ dev and $160$ confirmation worlds, the confirmation worlds all on held-out templates.
In the sixth round the confirmation worlds were deduplicated against every earlier render of the domain, including the fifth round's
multi-template confirmation set, and filled the held-out templates in turn. Templates whose only field besides the state is the object ran out of fresh
texts: \CLfourteenTmplWeekday{} (weekday), \CLfourteenTmplMonth{} (month) and \CLfourteenTmplHour{} (hour) held-out templates carry at
least ten worlds each, while \CLfourteenTmplSparse{} templates carry at most \CLfourteenTmplSparseMax{} worlds. The multi-template
results therefore speak for templates with several fields.
